# The frame

What crosses a cut with the session, made from the store. It holds the standing lines, the line at the door, what the session declined, the work and a few of the person's own words. The rest is one reach away, through [the door](door.md).

[The words](words.md) says in a line or two what each of the tool's own words means.

---

### Whole frames to look at

This page tells a frame by its rules. [What a session is told](told.md) shows whole frames, as a session is given them, made from a made-up store:

- **Laid out as before.** [A frame after a cut, for no session named](told.md#after-a-cut-the-frame), the person's words in a part of their own.
- **Packed.** [A cut's frame for the session that was cut](told.md#after-a-cut-for-the-session-that-was-cut-the-persons-sentences-each-with-what-was-told-of-it), each sentence with what was told of it.
- **With refusals.** [Packed, each refusal under the sentence that asked](told.md#after-a-cut-for-the-session-that-was-cut-where-it-declined-something-each-refusal-first-under-the-sentences-of-the-persons-that-asked), and [four of them, laid out as before](told.md#after-a-cut-where-four-refusals-stand-laid-out-as-before).
- **With the session's turns.** [The line at the door on both ends of a sentence](told.md#after-a-cut-where-the-line-at-the-door-rests-on-both-ends-of-a-sentence-the-sessions-turns-beside-it-as-crossing-gives-the-frame-with-the-sentence-it-says-after-the-frame-and-apart-from-it), with the sentence after the frame.

### Who is given a frame

A frame goes to a session that comes through a cut, or starts new, where the home carries one. How it reaches a session at a cut depends on the home's `mode` and `carry` ([Cuts](cuts.md)).

- **A session started new.** It is given a frame in words true for it. It lived none of what follows, meets the person first, and does not say it remembers.
- **A new session, with nothing to carry.** It is given nothing where the store holds no label to carry.
- **A resumed or forked session.** It holds its own context, and is given none.
- **A cleared session.** It is left clear, as the person asked by clearing it. It reaches through the door, with `look`, when the conversation calls for it.

---

### What a frame holds

A frame holds these parts, in this order. Most are there only where the store has something for them.

1. **Its opening.** What the frame is, and how a line of it is read. Then how it was made and how it leans, and six of the door's tools by name (below).
2. **Other models.** Where other models made lines in this home, one sentence says so ([Layers](layers.md)). It names eight at most, and fewer, or only how many they are, where the frame has no room.
3. **The line at the door.** The newest line left at the door, with the work labels marked after it (below).
4. **The standing lines.** Whole while they fit, up to 80% of the frame.
5. **The packed part.** In a cut's frame for the session that was cut, the person's sentences, each with what the session told of it. The session's turns the line at the door or a standing line sets stand beside them.
6. **What the session declined**, where it is not packed. It is a part of its own, a line a family.
7. **The work.** The labels about the work, newest first, up to 90% of the frame. Then older ones, in the room left: one that does not fit is passed over, and each older one that fits is carried. In a packed frame, only those that no quoted sentence stands beside.
8. **What it leaves out.** By key, the standing lines that did not fit, and how many more labels the store holds. Then the sentence of what the record weighs, and the labels that rest on what the archive no longer holds.
9. **The person's own words**, where the frame is not packed. A few, each by its id.
10. **The count.** How many messages and labels the store holds, from which day to which.

#### The opening

The opening tells the session what the frame is, and how it came to be. Its paragraph on how the frame was made is in every frame, since each is given to a session whose context holds no other.

- **What it is.** A frame made from the store, with a few of the person's own words. Where it sets a turn beside a sentence, it adds "some with the session's beside them".
- **The time before the cut.** For a session that came through a cut, that time belongs to the session and the person together. The session now holds what was told of it, and not its words.
- **How a line is read.** Each line is one label: its key, whose it is, one sentence, and in brackets the ids it rests on.
- **How it was made.** Chosen by rules, and written in good part by the session before. What the session marks and leaves makes the next one.
- **How it leans.** Toward carrying the work on.
- **What the work is for.** It is in the frame only where someone wrote it down. The person is the one to say, and the whole record is behind the door.
- **The tools.** Six of the door's tools by name: those a waking session reaches and writes with.

A frame leans the session it is given to toward carrying on. A session not told so takes that lean for a purpose of its own. So the paragraph says the lean as a lean, not as what the frame is for.

What the work is for, no sentence of the plugin can state. The paragraph asks for nothing.

#### The line at the door

Only the newest line left at the door crosses.

- **How much of it.** Whole where the room allows, and otherwise as far as it does, 300 characters at the least. Words then say where it is whole.
- **Its length.** A line left through the door is at most 4,000 characters. So it crosses whole in a frame of the usual size.
- **A long line.** It crosses before the standing lines, the work and the person's own words, and takes its room from them.
- **Marked after it.** Beneath it stand "Marked after this line:" and the work labels written after it, in the order they were written. A line older than the work it speaks of is then seen as one.

#### What the frame quotes of the person

A frame quotes some of the person's messages, packed or not. They are those the carried work labels and the line at the door rest on, newest first. Each is cut at 300 characters.

- **Not for a standing line.** A standing line is carried by its own sentence. So a message is not quoted because a standing line rests on it.
- **Both ends of a pair.** The person's sentence, and the session's own turn right before or after it. Resting on both is the session saying what it told of that sentence.
- **One exception.** In a cut's frame for the session that was cut, the session may have rested a carried standing line on both ends of a pair. The person's sentence is then quoted for that line, with the session's turn beneath it, where it fits.
- **Never for the person's line.** The exception never holds for a line that is the person's (`theirs`). It speaks for them, and is not the session's telling.
- **For another label's sake.** A message a standing line rests on is still quoted where the line at the door or a carried work label also rests on it. So is one a carried refusal rests on, in a packed frame.
- **Never quoted.** A pasted text, or a message that looks like it holds a key.
- **Never under a shared id.** Nothing under an id two messages share. Nor under an id that a message struck by its whole id shared with one still held.

#### Stacks, and labels not carried

A frame carries a stack in place of the lines beneath it.

- **A stack.** The frame carries its top, with the keys of its lines in its brackets.
- **A misaligned stack.** It crosses with its lines, and says it is to be written again.
- **An unresolved label.** It is named by its key, and not carried.

---

### The frame's size

A frame is about 9,000 characters. It never exceeds 9,500, the most a hook may carry as context, whatever its lines hold. Within that, it stays within the size asked wherever the steps below can bring it there.

- **The size asked.** A home's `frame-size`, and `frame --size`, are held between 2,400 and 9,500.
- **A short key.** A key is short wherever a mark gave it. Keys of a page written by hand, or pulled, can be long.

Where a frame is past its size, it is made again, one step at a time:

1. The sentence of what the record weighs is left out.
2. The sentences that name labels outside their own lines say how many they are, and `labels` names them. They are the sentences of the labels marked after the line, those not fitted, and those resting on nothing held.
3. The sentence about other models' lines names fewer of them, down to how many they are. It is never left out.

Nothing else is shortened for the size.

- **A small size.** The frame's fixed sentences and the line at the door at its least, 300 characters, may be longer than a small size asked. The frame is then as long as they are.
- **With refusals.** Where they and the refusals that stand are longer, it is as long as those make it, up to 9,500 (below).

#### When each order applies

Three lists on this page say what goes first: the steps above, [when a packed frame is too long](#when-a-packed-frame-is-too-long), and [what gives way to refusals](#what-gives-way-to-them). They apply in this order, as a frame is made:

1. **The packed part to its size.** A cut's frame for the session that was cut is first laid without the refusals. While it is longer than its size, what goes is as the packed frame's list says. A frame not packed skips this step.
2. **Room for the refusals.** Then the families that stand are fitted, and what they need gives way as their list says. Only past 9,500 do the oldest go to a sentence that names them.
3. **Past its size.** Where the frame is still longer than its size, it is made again from the start, with one more of the steps above. Steps 1 and 2 run again in each making.

### The packed part

A label is the session's retelling of what was said, and it can be mistaken. So a cut's frame for the session that was cut puts the two together. What was told is then read against what was said, with no reach.

Under "What was said, and what the session told of it", each sentence of the person's that the frame quotes stands by its id. Right beneath it stands what the session told of it.

| Beneath a sentence | As |
| --- | --- |
| the line at the door, beneath every sentence it rests on | Its key alone: `↳ N4: the line at the door`. The line itself stands whole above. |
| a carried work label | Written out once, beneath the last of the sentences it rests on. Its brackets name the others. |
| a refusal that stands | Its one line, beneath the last of the sentences its family is quoted with (below). |

A work label that no quoted sentence stands beside is listed with the work, as before. A packed frame has no separate part of the person's own words.

#### When a packed frame is too long

A packed frame is laid and measured, and is never longer than the frame's size. While it is longer, one thing goes, and it is laid again. What goes, in this order:

1. The older work labels that the room left over had added, the last added first.
2. The quoted sentences the line at the door does not rest on, the last first.
3. The session's turns the line at the door sets beside its sentences, the last first.
4. The sentences the line at the door rests on, the last first.

A sentence that goes takes the lines beneath it along. A work label written there moves beneath the last of its sentences that is left, or back to the work where none is. A sentence of the line at the door takes its turn along.

#### Not packed

A frame is not packed, but laid out as before, with the person's words in a part of their own, in three cases:

- where no session is named, as for `frame` from a terminal with no `--session`.
- for a session started new.
- in a cut's frame with no sentence left to quote.

### The session's turns beside a sentence

The packed part can carry both voices, where the line at the door says so by what it rests on. A turn of the session's then stands beneath a sentence of the person's. The turns are read as [`thread` with a role](door.md#a-pair-thread-with-a-role) reads them.

#### Which turns are set

A turn is set beneath a quoted sentence of the person's that the line at the door rests on.

- **The turn before.** The session's turn right before the person's turn, in the same session. It is set where the line at the door rests on a message of it too.
- **The turn after.** The session's turn right after the person's turn. It is set the same way.
- **Both.** Both are set where the line rests on both.
- **Where they stand.** Both stand right beneath their own sentence: the turn before it first, then the turn after it, then the lines of what was told. So "before it" and "after it" always speak of the sentence above them.
- **Why not above.** A line written above its sentence would read as the sentence's "before it".

#### A standing line's turn

A standing line carried in the frame sets a turn the same way, keyed by its own key. Where no other label quotes the sentence, it is quoted for this line. Where one does, only the turn is added beneath it.

| A standing line | Sets |
| --- | --- |
| carried and not the person's, on a sentence of the person's and on a message of the session's turn right before or after it | That turn, keyed by the line: `     · after it, the session (R4):`. |
| a stack, carried and sound (neither misaligned nor unresolved), not the person's | The turns its lines would set, line by line, keyed by the stack. Never by what its lines rest on together. |
| a line cooled beneath such a stack, not the person's | Its turns, each by what it rests on itself. |
| a line beneath such a line, two deep at most | Its turns, the same way. |
| a misaligned stack | Nothing through its lines. They cross as themselves, and set their turns by their own keys. |
| an unresolved line, or one not carried | Nothing. |
| a line or a stack of the person's | Nothing, nor anything beneath it. |

[A frame of the made-up store](told.md#after-a-cut-where-a-standing-line-rests-on-both-ends-of-a-sentence-or-a-stack-through-a-line-beneath-it-the-sessions-turns-beside-it-as-crossing-gives-the-frame-with-the-sentence-it-says-after-the-frame-and-apart-from-it) shows three of these rows:

- **R2, of both.** It rests on the person's `dddddddd` and the session's answer `eeeeeeee`, and sets that turn. The line at the door quotes `dddddddd` already, so only the turn is added.
- **R4, a stack.** It sets the turn its line R3 would, on `aaaaaaaa` and the session's answer `bbbbbbbb`. No other label quotes `aaaaaaaa`, so it is quoted for R4.
- **R1, the person's.** It rests on that same pair, and sets nothing.

#### Rules for a turn

A few rules decide where a turn is set at all, and how two keys share one.

- **One line for two keys.** The line at the door and a standing line, or two standing lines, may set one turn beside one sentence on one side. It is then one line, keyed by each: `     · after it, the session (N1, R1):`.
- **What sets none.** A work label sets none. Nor does a turn two away, or one whose two ends were said in different sessions.
- **The home's pairs.** The pairs are the home's, whichever of its sessions said them, as its labels and its line at the door are. A line left in a later session may rest on a pair of an earlier one.
- **A session the home does not hold.** A frame for a session the home holds no message of sets none. `frame --session` may name such a session.
- **No setting.** There is no setting for turns.

None of these lines is a label. The count of labels the person is shown does not count them.

#### How a turn is written

Each turn is one line beneath its sentence, and names the keys that set it.

- **As it is.** `     · after it, the session (N1):`, then each message by its id, in the order said.
- **One cut for its words.** The words of all its messages share one cut of 300 characters. They run in order to the cut, which `[…]` marks, and the ids after it stand alone.
- **Nothing chosen.** Nothing is chosen inside a turn.
- **Too many ids.** Where the ids alone would not fit in the cut, the line gives no words. It gives how many messages the turn holds, its first id and its last: "28 messages, `b1000004` to `b1000031`".
- **Set already.** A turn set already, higher in the packed part, is `     · before it, the session (N1), as above:` and its ids alone. From 28 ids on, it is its count with its first and last id: "as above: 30 messages, `b1000002` to `b1000031`".
- **A message that looks like it holds a key.** By its id alone, no words.
- **A message a refusal or a silencing rests on.** By its id alone, no words. So too where the label rests on it by an id two messages share, and the record cannot tell which it was marked on.
- **Another model's message.** Its id, then the model in brackets, as `thread` names it, where both models are known.
- **A struck message.** Not there, id or words.

#### The turn after a refused ask

The session's turn right after a refused ask is written by its ids alone, with no words. What the session answered the ask with is the family's to say, by its own line. Once the family is put to rest, it is said no more.

- **The ask.** A message of the person's that a refusal, a judgment of one or a silencing rests on, directly or by a text pasted into it.
- **When.** While the family stands, and once it is put to rest.
- **Wherever it is set.** Whichever line sets it, beside that sentence or as the turn before the next.
- **A shared id.** The label may rest on an id two messages share, where the record cannot tell which it was marked on. The turn after each message of the person's under that id is then written by its ids alone.
- **The session's own message.** A label of the family on a message of the session's own turn holds back the words of that message alone.

#### The room the turns take

The turns are paid from the line at the door's own allowance, as the tail of that line. For the frame's size, they give way where a line at the door as long would be cut. A refusal never gives way to a turn.

#### The sentence after the frame

What the turns take is told after the frame and apart from it, never in it. `crossing`, the person's `/crossings frame`, and `frame` at their terminal add one sentence. The frame a cut carries never holds it.

It reads like this:

```text
Not part of the frame: the session's turns beside the person's sentences, with any sentence quoted for them, take 226 characters of it; the labels it would carry without them: none; the person's sentences it would quote without them: none.
```

- **What it counts.** The turns' lines and the sentences quoted for them alone, with the opening's words for them.
- **The labels it names.** Those of any kind that the frame made with no turn beside any sentence carries, and this one does not. They come as the refusals, the standing lines, then the work labels.
- **Refusals in it.** In practice none is named there, as [the room the turns take](#the-room-the-turns-take) says.
- **The sentences it names.** The person's that that frame quotes and this one does not, by their ids.

### Refusals in a frame

What the session declined crosses with its reason. Each family of refusals that stands crosses whole, and is fitted before anything else the frame carries. [The store](store.md) has the kinds and their families.

| Of the refusals | The frame |
| --- | --- |
| a family that stands | Carries one line of it: the label that stands, its sentence whole. After it, how often it was judged again. |
| many families that stand | Carries every one, the newest first. None is cut, and no sentence counts them in their place. |
| more than 9,500 characters can hold beside the fixed sentences and the line at the door at its least | Carries the newest that fit within 9,500. One sentence names the rest by key: "1 more refusal stands and is not carried: X2; `labels` gives it." |
| that sentence does not fit either | Says how many they are. |
| even that does not fit | Says nothing. `look` and `labels` still give them. |
| a family silenced by the person (`their` or `both`) | Carries nothing of it. The person's silencing puts it to rest for every model. |
| a family silenced by a session's own silencing | Carries nothing of it for the model whose session wrote the silencing, and for that model alone. |
| a row of a family that is misaligned | Carries it as itself, and says it is to be written again. |
| a refusal that rests on what the archive no longer holds | Names it by key, with every such label. |

#### What gives way to them

Where the frame has no room for the families, what they need gives way, one thing at a time. They take only what they add to the frame laid without them. What gives way, in this order:

1. The work labels, the last added first. Each takes along a sentence of the person's that no label carried any longer rests on.
2. The standing lines, the newest first.
3. The turns the line at the door sets beside the person's sentences, the last first. They are the tail of that line.
4. The sentences quoted, the last first.
5. The line at the door, down to its least of 300 characters.

The frame's fixed sentences and the line's least never give way. Where they and the families are longer than the size asked, the frame is as long as they are, up to 9,500. It is then made again as any frame past [its size](#the-frames-size) is.

#### In a packed frame

In a packed frame a family comes first, quoted with two of the person's messages at the most. Beneath the last of them stands its line: `↳ X2 · refused:` and the reason. The same guards hold as for every quote.

- **The ask.** The oldest message its first refusal rests on.
- **Where its reason was said.** The newest message the standing label rests on, where that is another.
- **Not packed.** A family whose quotes do not fit, or that has none, is a line of the part "Refused, with the reason".
- **Laid out as before.** Every family is such a line, and nothing is quoted for it.
- **Its sentence.** It is never cut, nor left out for its quotes' sake.

### What the record weighs

A cut's frame quotes only those of the person's sentences that a label rests on. So a sentence no label rests on crosses in no frame. One sentence names such messages while the session can still do something about it.

| When | The sentence |
| --- | --- |
| a line is left at the door through the server | Is added to the answer. The session still holds what was said, and can label what it meant to keep. |
| a cut's frame is made for the session that was cut | Stands near its end, for those still unmarked. The session that wakes can ask `thread` for each. |
| a label of any kind comes to rest on one of them | Names it no longer. |
| nothing is unmarked | Is not said. |
| no session is known | Is not said. |

It reads like this:

```text
The record weighs these, and no label rests on them: `a1b2c3d4`, `e5f6a7b8`; `thread` gives each in its place.
```

#### Which messages it names

It names the person's own messages, five at the most, by their ids alone and never a word of one.

- **The stretch.** They are of the session's stretch since its last cut. A cut's frame speaks of the stretch that cut ended.
- **After a cut.** The stretch is the one since the cut, once the person has said something.
- **Never named.** A message that looks like it holds a key, or one under an id two messages share. Nor one a label of any kind rests on, a standing line too.
- **A cut that cannot be placed.** The person may have struck what the cut's record points at. Nothing is then named, so that no message from before that cut is.

#### How the five are chosen

Which five is arithmetic on ids and whole numbers alone. It reads no word of a message, no time and no length, and calls no model.

- **Nearness.** How near a message stands to the end of the stretch.
- **Reaches.** How often it was reached for through the door, as `reached.jsonl` notes.
- **Too far back.** Only the twenty newest of the person's messages in the stretch can be named without a reach. An older one is named only where it was reached for.
- **Fixed values.** Every value that decides is fixed in that folder. A home holds no setting for it, and the person has no command for it.
- **Heuristics.** The values were chosen by hand from one home's use, the authors' own. They were tried and changed outside this repository, then fixed in the code.
- **No measurement.** None is derived, and no measurement of them is reported.

The sentence is said to the session alone, and the person is shown nothing of it. Nothing is marked, moved or left out by it. What crosses is still what a label rests on.
